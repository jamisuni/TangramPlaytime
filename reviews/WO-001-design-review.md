## Findings

### F1 — CONTRACT · Blocker · `Silhouette.outlineCorners` (TASK-001) and §3 "Inherited blind spot (out of scope)"
- **Observation:** The design decides "corner" by the angle sum alone (not a corner when |Σ−π| ≤ 1e-6 or |Σ−2π| ≤ 1e-6) and scopes out the case "two 90° corners meeting in opposite quadrants" with the claim "No shipped puzzle has one (V5). If a test meets one, it is a finding, not a fix." The claim is false. In `Tangrams/shapes-warmup-4.json` (kind `warmup`, difficulty 1 — listed right after the minis) the vertex (2,2) is shared by LT2 `(2,2),(6,2),(4,4)` (covers 0–45°), MT `(2,2),(2,0),(4,2)` (270–360°) and ST2 `(2,2),(2,4),(1,3)` (90–135°): the sum is 180° but the covered directions are two separate wedges (45°–90° and 135°–270° are both outside the silhouette). The boundary turns at (2,2); it is a corner of the silhouette outline under REQ-019 ("a corner of the silhouette outline") and under `ILockEngine.outlineCorners`'s own definition ("a point of the silhouette's boundary … where the boundary turns"). The design's rule omits it, so (i) `outlineCorners(warmup-4)` is wrong against the Contract, and (ii) a piece whose 45° or 135° corner sits on (2,2) with no other corner on an anchor is a valid REQ-019 position that returns `None` (REQ-019.A1, and the REQ-019 rationale "the uncovered region always has a convex corner, that corner is always an anchor" fails there). V5 does not prevent this: it rejects corner-only *connectivity*, not a pinch point inside an edge-connected silhouette. `design-inputs.md` §2 "Also fine" endorses the angle-sum rule, but §2 states that the Contract wins over any input. (Note: in the stored solution each of the three pieces at (2,2) also touches another recognised corner, so the stored-solution lock sequence still works; alternative covers and the REQ-019 guarantee do not.)
- **Proposed resolution:** Keep the port but change the classification in `Silhouette.outlineCorners` from the angle *sum* to the covered *directions*: for a vertex, collect the angular arcs covered at that point (a piece having it as a vertex covers the arc between its two neighbours; a piece having it strictly inside an edge covers a half-plane); the vertex is **not** a corner iff the union of the arcs is the full circle or one contiguous arc of exactly 180° (tolerance 1e-6 rad); otherwise it is a corner. This reproduces the reference on all 13 puzzles except the one point above, which becomes a corner. Rewrite the §3 bullet accordingly (the blind spot is fixed in the kernel; `tools/tangram_geom.outline_corners` and its V11 build-order line keep it — out of WO-001's scope, a note for the planner to carry to `proposals.md` / the content WO), and tell the kit author that `tangram_geom` is **not** an oracle for `outlineCorners` on `shapes-warmup-4`. A kit line "warmup-4 (2,2) is an outline corner" pins the fix. If instead the orchestrator decides the angle-sum rule *is* the Contract's meaning, that is a `decisions.md` row plus a `chg-draft` for REQ-019/`ILockEngine` — not a silent design assumption.
- **Triage:** **accepted — fixed, verified 2026-10-11 (second look, F1 only).** The `Silhouette` row (design 13,770 B) now classifies by contiguity: per polygon the arc between its two edge directions at p that holds the direction to its centre (a half-plane for a point strictly inside an edge), arcs sorted by start, gaps = (next.start − cur.end) mod 2π counted in (1e-6, 2π − 1e-6); not a corner iff Σ ≥ 2π − 1e-6 or (exactly one gap and |Σ − π| ≤ 1e-6). Re-derived with a literal scratchpad implementation of that wording (`check_f1_fix.py`, `python -I`) over all 13 shipped puzzles against `tangram_geom.outline_corners`: identical corner sets on 12 (cat 12, mountain 3, mini-1 3, mini-2 4, rectangle 4, square 4, warmup-1 16, warmup-2 12, warmup-3 15, arrow 11, house 9, sailboat 7); `shapes-warmup-4` 13 → 14, adding exactly (2,2) (arcs 0–45°, 90–135°, 270–360° → two gaps) and dropping nothing. The `outlineCorners` seam row pins "warmup-4 includes (2,2)"; the §3 bullet is rewritten (reference tool keeps the blind spot; the kit must not use it as the oracle there); the `atan2` note is correct ((−π, π], normalise to [0, 2π)). Blocker closed.

### F2 — TEST · Should · Kit fixtures row, REQ-019.A3 (TASK-003) and §3 "A3"
- **Observation:** The proposed A3 fixture — ST at turn 0 centred (2, 1/3) on the Gift box, vertices (1,0), (3,0), (2,1) — returns `None` under the correct engine, but it also returns `None` under the two plausible "hidden anchor" bugs the criterion exists to catch: anchors = every solution vertex (unfiltered 180°/360° points), and anchors = outline-edge midpoints. Every vertex of that ST is ≥ 1.0 from every solution vertex and every midpoint, so the fixture cannot fail. Re-derived with the design's own search: at centre (2, 4/3) (vertices (1,1), (3,1), (2,2) — two corners on 360° solution vertices) the correct engine returns `None` and the all-vertices bug **locks**; at turn 6 centred (10/3, 2) (vertices (3,3), (3,1), (4,2) — on a 360° vertex and the 180° edge point (4,2)) the correct engine returns `None` and **both** bugs lock.
- **Proposed resolution:** Replace or complement the A3 fixture with a pose whose corners sit on non-corner solution vertices and on a straight edge point — the two above are verified — so that A3 distinguishes "no hidden anchors" from "solution vertices are anchors". Keep the design's note that the piece is inside the silhouette (so only the anchor rule can reject it).

### F3 — TEST · Should · Kit fixtures row, `poseOf` and REQ-022.A1 (TASK-003)
- **Observation:** (a) `poseOf` "finds the unique turn 0..7 (and mirrored = true for PG only) … fails loudly when none matches" — the turn is not unique: every SQ polygon matches four turns (order-4 symmetry: Gift box SQ → 0, 2, 4, 6), every PG two (Chocolate bar PG → (3, mirrored) and (7, mirrored); Gift box PG → 0 and 4). A kit written to the word "unique" would fail on every puzzle that has a square. (b) The REQ-022.A1 fixture ("an arrangement different from the stored solution that fills the silhouette") is not named, and `isSolved` is a pure set check, so a test that hands it seven hand-built `Placement`s passes for *any* seven placements and asserts nothing about covering. Re-derived: the Gift-box solution rotated 90° clockwise about (2,2) differs from the stored solution in all seven pieces and locks piece by piece through `findLock` in tray order (each drop 0.22 away); the Mini pyramid has exactly one exact cover and cannot serve A1.
- **Proposed resolution:** (a) Specify `poseOf` as "the lowest matching turn (mirrored only for PG); fails loudly when none matches", and note that SQ matches four and PG two. (b) Name the A1 arrangement (Gift box rotated 90° about (2,2), or any other verified cover) and require the kit to reach it through seven `findLock` calls — asserting each lock and `isSolved` false before the seventh, true after — so the test can fail on a geometry bug.
- **Triage:** *(filled on recirculation)*

### F4 — QUALITY · Should · §3 "Oracle honesty A1" and §1 step 5 (TASK-002/003)
- **Observation:** TYPE-004 says nothing about ties; the design breaks exact score ties by generation order (corner i, then anchor j, after the stable sort by d) with a 1e-9 comparison. That is a deterministic, sensible choice, but it is a value decision the kit must not accidentally pin the other way — and the design's A1 warning ("a snugger valid candidate up to 0.12 away wins legitimately") does not say how the kit author should *choose* fixtures so that the intended pose is the unique best. The §3 line "anchors 1.5 and √2 are only 0.086 apart" has no referent in the three named puzzles (all Chocolate-bar coordinates are multiples of √2/2).
- **Proposed resolution:** State the tie rule as a kit constraint ("fixtures must have a unique best score by > 1e-9; a tie is a fixture defect, not an assertion target") and either name where 1.5 vs √2 arises or drop the line. The planner can carry this in TASK-003's brief; no design change needed.
